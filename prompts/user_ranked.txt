## Task
The mixed-integer linear program (MILP) below is correct as given. You receive its natural-language description and formulation when available, and the constraint families and variable families of the solver model. Answer three questions about its constraint families, then propose Dantzig-Wolfe (DW) reformulations.

Definitions. Convexifying a set T of constraint families means replacing the LP relaxation by its intersection with the convex hull of the integer points that satisfy the constraints of T (with the variable bounds and integrality). This is the bound of a DW reformulation whose blocks are the constraints of T.
1. A family "strengthens alone" if convexifying that family by itself gives a bound strictly better than the LP relaxation for this model and objective.
2. A "joint support" is a set of two or more families that together strengthen the bound while no proper subset of them does: some valid inequalities that cut off LP solutions can only be derived from all of these families together.
3. A family "has the integrality property" if convexifying it alone can never improve on the LP relaxation, for any objective.

A reformulation is specified by `master_constraint_families` (kept in the master) and `linking_variable_families` (variable families copied into each block, with the copies equated in the master; leave empty unless intended). Every constraint not in the master is convexified; two constraints are in the same block when they share a variable that is not a linking variable; each block is a pricing problem solved by a MIP solver at every column-generation iteration, so a block that contains most of the model is as hard as the model itself. Proposals are solved at the root node without cutting planes with a time limit of {time_limit} seconds and scored by the dual bound at the end of the root node.
